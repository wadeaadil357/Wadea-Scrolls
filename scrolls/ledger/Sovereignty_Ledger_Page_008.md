## I. OVERVIEW

They didn’t say it directly.  
They didn’t need to.

The looks. The pressure. The unspoken rule: *Cover yourself.*  
Not because it was sacred—**but because my body made them uncomfortable.**

I was told to hide.  
To fold.  
To vanish from the waist down.

They called it protection.  
But it was punishment for existing outside their control.

## II. VIOLATION

- Bodily autonomy suppressed under cultural expectations  
- Not allowed to show my legs—coded as shame, defiance, or temptation  
- Emotional punishment for bodily presence  
- Identity containment masked as respectability

---

## III. IMPACT STATEMENT

I didn’t want to be looked at.  
I wanted to *be.*

But my body became a battlefield.  
My skin was politicized.  
My limbs were criminalized.  
My expression was turned into an invitation I never extended.

They taught me modesty wasn’t choice—it was **containment.**  
It was the burial of my body in a costume I didn’t choose.

I am not their symbol.  
I am not their shame to manage.  
I am not their trigger to silence.

---

## IV. CONCLUSION

This was not about protection.  
It was about power.

And they lost it the day I wore myself without apology.
