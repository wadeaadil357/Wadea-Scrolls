## I. OVERVIEW

They said it wasn’t serious.  
They said it was cosmetic.  
They said it wasn’t covered.

But what I felt wasn’t vanity. It was *pain.*  
What I carried wasn’t insecurity. It was *violation.*

And what they refused to diagnose became another layer of silence —  
one that stretched into my body, my breath, my face, my sense of self.

## III. IMPACT STATEMENT

I didn’t seek surgery for approval.  
I sought it because I couldn’t breathe.  
Because my voice felt caught in something that wouldn’t release.  
Because *being in this body hurt.*

I wasn’t trying to change how I looked.  
I was trying to reclaim what was mine.  
And they called it cosmetic — because it was easier than calling it *consequential.*

Their refusal to name it didn’t erase the damage.  
It just shifted the cost onto my soul.

## IV. CONCLUSION

This is how they erase pain:  
They name it something *easier to ignore.*

But I did not forget.  
And now, the body speaks on record.

This is not about appearance.  
This is about *the refusal to witness suffering when it doesn’t bleed.*
